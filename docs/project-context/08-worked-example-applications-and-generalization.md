# Worked Example, Applications, and Generalization

## Canonical worked example: Northern Fortress / House Vale

This example exists to demonstrate the architecture through a small disruption of an authored trajectory.

### Authored trajectory

```text
A. Northern Fortress holds
        ->
B. Royal army redeploys east
        ->
C. Participant is sent to negotiate with House Vale
        ->
D. Vale joins the Crown
        ->
E. Rebellion is isolated
```

Relevant Enclaves may include:

- Northern Army;
- Royal Court;
- House Vale;
- Merchant Guild;
- Northern Province.

Relevant Combinatorial Enclaves can be introduced where the example needs more precise populations.

The author establishes a private military weakness at the fortress, initially available only through appropriate military/command structures.

### Authoritative disruption

The fortress falls before the expected redeployment.

That becomes an authoritative Event.

The Event changes world state and creates canonical information such as:

> Northern Fortress has fallen.

The Event being authoritative does not make every Actor aware of it.

### Diffusion and transformation

A surviving soldier receives or observes information about the fall.

The soldier tells a sibling.

The sibling is connected to the Merchant Guild.

The sibling summarizes the report:

> The rebels have broken the northern defenses.

A merchant carries that south.

Another Actor exaggerates it:

> The entire northern army has been destroyed.

The architecture can now preserve simultaneously:

- the authoritative Event;
- a canonical Fact spawned by the Event;
- a reliable report;
- a summary;
- an exaggeration/false claim;
- different Actor exposures;
- different Actor Memories.

If the report becomes sufficiently widespread within a relevant Enclave, the example may also demonstrate:

- Saturation;
- Institutional Knowledge;
- Institutional Emission.

This can show how information moves from individual transmission into higher-level institutional propagation without becoming automatically canonical.

### Lady Vale as a bounded Cognitive Actor

Lady Vale should exist persistently whether or not a model is currently reasoning for her.

Her persistent subjective state may include:

- authored personality/history;
- existing Knowledge;
- prior Memories;
- House Vale Enclave/Rank relationships;
- relationship history with the Participant;
- current political goals;
- local reports;
- false or incomplete information.

When cognition is triggered, the narrative agent attached to Lady Vale receives a **temporary context constructed from her legitimate subjective state**, not omniscient world state.

It should not receive:

- hidden canonical military information she never encountered;
- the author's intended trajectory;
- private Memories belonging to other Actors;
- future Events.

This is the key Section 12 demonstration.

### Participant intervention

The Participant reaches House Vale before the Royal Court's official messenger and argues that the Crown is losing.

Lady Vale's narrative agent interprets this from her own bounded perspective and may decide that she should:

- delay the alliance;
- order scouts north;
- question the Participant;
- seek independent confirmation;
- take another plausible action.

The agent chooses what Lady Vale **attempts**.

It does not authoritatively decide what happens.

### Authoritative resolution

Lady Vale's attempted actions return to the authoritative system.

The world resolves:

- whether orders can be issued;
- whether scouts can depart;
- what resources/conditions apply;
- what new Interactions occur;
- what Events are accepted;
- what Facts/Knowledge become available;
- which Memories are created.

The resulting Events change:

- House Vale's diplomatic state;
- Crown support;
- military options;
- information available to Vale Actors/scouts;
- eligibility/meaning of later authored developments.

The intended trajectory becomes something more like:

```text
A -> X -> C' -> Y -> ?
```

The system does not generate an unconstrained replacement story. It computes consequence inside the authored world.

## What the example should demonstrate

The final worked example should explicitly trace:

- authored trajectory;
- authoritative state;
- seven foundational primitives;
- key derivatives where relevant;
- canonical and non-canonical Facts;
- provenance;
- Enclave/contextual access;
- Combinatorial Enclave where useful;
- local diffusion;
- information transformation;
- Saturation / Institutional Knowledge / Emission if space allows;
- persistent Memory;
- bounded Actor subjective state;
- temporary context construction;
- narrative-agent reasoning;
- attempted action;
- authoritative Event resolution;
- persistent physical and informational consequence;
- reactive narrative divergence.

## Applications

Possible applications already identified:

- role-playing games;
- immersive simulations;
- strategy games with persistent characters/factions;
- interactive fiction;
- live-service narrative worlds;
- educational/training simulations;
- hybrid tabletop/digital experiences;
- author-operated live narrative environments.

## Live authoring

An author or Gamemaster could intervene during play:

- add new authored material;
- alter future pressures;
- create new conditional developments;
- respond to unexpected emergent conditions.

This adds a dimension of live in-play development rarely available at scale.

It is an application, not a dependency.

## Different generation levels

Enclave is not tied to heavy generative use.

Possible implementations range from:

- fully deterministic simulation using the same ontology/information/causal architecture;
- models used only for dialogue realization;
- models used for interpretation and Actor decisions;
- specialized decision models + conventional rendering;
- small/local models for routine cognition;
- larger models only for narratively difficult situations;
- mixed deterministic/probabilistic Cognitive Actors.

The architectural claim is broader than one model stack.

## Generalization beyond narrative

The architecture may generalize to controlled information environments containing:

- persistent Actors;
- authoritative state;
- asymmetric information;
- Event-driven change;
- controlled exposure;
- bounded cognition;
- external/internal decision makers.

Potential non-game uses should be explored cautiously and separately from the main narrative thesis so the paper does not lose focus.

A useful generalized concept remains:

> an agent-native environment layer

The environment, rather than only the agent's memory system, is structured so that autonomous Actors can safely and coherently operate against persistent state.
